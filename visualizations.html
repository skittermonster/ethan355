Video Game Sales Analysis

Exploring Nintendo-era game sales through Vega-Lite visualizations.

The dataset (videogames_wide.csv, 16,598 rows) captures global video game sales by title, platform, genre, publisher, release year, and four regional sales figures (millions of units).

Global Sales Snapshot

  • Mean: 0.54M copies per title
  • Median: 0.17M (skewed long tail)
  • Mode: 0.01M (many niche releases)
  • Range: 0.01–82.74M copies
  • Std Dev: 1.56M

Top Contributors

  • Genre: Action (1.75B units)
  • Platform: PlayStation 2 (1.26B units)
  • Publisher: Nintendo (1.79B units)
  • Peak Release Year: 2009

Regional Averages

  • North America: 0.26M
  • Europe: 0.15M
  • Japan: 0.08M
  • Other Regions: 0.05M

1. Global Sales by Genre and Platform

I filtered to the eight platforms with the largest lifetime sales and compared them against the nine genres in the dataset. A heatmap makes it easy to spot strong genre–platform pairings.

Design Reflection

The rect heatmap emphasizes relative dominance without overwhelming viewers with hundreds of individual titles. Color encoding highlights how Sports and Action titles drove the PlayStation 2 and Wii, while handheld platforms (DS, PSP) lean into Role-Playing and Puzzle experiences.

2. Sales Over Time by Platform and Genre

To study momentum, I plotted yearly global sales for the five highest-selling platforms and grouped the view by major genres. Small multiples prevent the lines from overlapping excessively while keeping the roles of platform and genre visible.

Design Reflection

The faceted line charts show how Action and Shooter genres peaked during the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 era, then faded after 2012. Meanwhile Role-Playing games maintain steadier sales on DS and Wii, underscoring Nintendo’s longer-tail support for these genres.

3. Regional Sales vs. Platform

Each bar stacks the four recorded regions for a platform, revealing how geography shaped console popularity. Hover tooltips expose absolute values while color encodes region.

Design Reflection

The stacked bars call out that Xbox 360 and PlayStation platforms lean heavily on North America and Europe, while Nintendo handhelds (DS, 3DS, Game Boy Advance) register a much larger share of Japanese sales. This validates the common view that handheld gaming resonates most in Japan.

4. Story: Japan’s Love for Role-Playing Games

After exploring the broad trends, I wanted to quantify which genres rely most on Japan. The scatter plot aggregates each genre’s lifetime global units and the proportion sold in Japan.

Design Reflection

Positioning genres by total global sales (x-axis) and their Japan share (y-axis) makes the outliers obvious. Role-Playing games sell fewer copies globally than Action, but almost 38% of their sales come from Japan, more than double any other major genre. That insight would have been hidden in a simple ranking table.
